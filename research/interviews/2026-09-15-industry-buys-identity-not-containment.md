# Interview: The industry keeps buying identity when it needed containment

**Source:** backlog item 2026-09-14 — The industry keeps buying identity when it needed containment
**Status:** Awaiting answers

## The idea in three sentences

Three security vendors — CrowdStrike, Proofpoint, and Tenable — shipped agent-identity products in the same seven-day window in early September 2026, and at RSAC 2026 CrowdStrike's own CTO went on record saying observing an agent's actions is solvable but inferring its intent isn't, because IAM was built for human sessions with a clear start and end, not agents that act continuously and chain actions across services. A concurrent survey found 94% of organizations believe their agents have the right access, but only 33% enforce least privilege, and of the enterprises that did give each agent a scoped identity, fewer than one in five also isolate it — the same swap "Wallet is not a conscience" made about one company's product (proving identity answers "is this authentic," not "should I trust what it does") now showing up industry-wide, backed by a named executive instead of just the author's inference. The strongest counter is that three vendors shipping in one week is a normal competitive cluster, not proof of confusion — sophisticated buyers may already procure identity and containment as two separate, correctly labeled line items, and the "gap" could be ordinary rollout lag rather than the category mistake the wallet post predicted.
