I keep running into products wearing "具身智能" — embodied intelligence — like a sticker, and it's starting to bug me. Nobody stops on what the phrase is actually claiming: an intelligence that happens to be embodied. As I keep telling people, the focus is intelligence, not the body. Most of what earns the label these days barely clears the second half, let alone the first.

Take 亲宝宝's "AI睡眠舱," an AI-branded baby crib that [went on sale in July 2026](https://www.stdaily.com/web/gdxw/2026-08/19/content_566388.html), pitched as completing a "perception-decision-execution-feedback" (感知-决策-执行-反馈) closed loop entirely inside the crib. That's real embodied-intelligence vocabulary, bolted onto a crib, in a year when [China's embodied-intelligence sector has already minted close to twenty new ¥10-billion-plus unicorns](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2026-07-14/doc-inihtvuh8554773.shtml). The vocabulary is worth money. Everyone wants to be caught using it.

The test I actually apply is the same one I used for ["AI-native game" is a test, not a vibe](/posts/ai-native-game-is-a-test): pull the thing out and see what's left. Take the intelligence back out of the crib. Does it still do its job for the user, the same way? Even if you remove the intelligence part and it still serves the user, that's not a loop. It's a sensor and a preset rule wearing a bigger word. The crib fails that test in exactly this direction: swap the algorithm for a plain timer and it still rocks the baby on schedule. People have generalized the term into hardware with a bit of software, and the hardware doesn't even have to be electronic. It can be any physical object with a story attached.

Where my own test gets less clean: once the thing driving the behavior is an unexplainably huge complex model, I'm honestly not sure the line holds. Something similar happened with large language models. Nobody voted on it, but at some point the models got big enough that people quietly stopped treating "intelligence" as a metaphor and started treating it as a description. Maybe a big enough model behind a body earns the same pass, whether or not the loop is doing the work I think it should. I don't have a settled answer, and I don't yet have one specific product where I can honestly say I can't tell if it's a real loop or a well-rehearsed demo. If you've got one, I want to hear it.

What bothers me about the current mess isn't only the crib. Embodied AI, physical AI, smart hardware, IoT, and plain robotics all get flattened into one word, and the flattening does real work, for a pitch deck, a valuation, a headline. And if I'm honest, there's a small sense of jealousy in there too: I don't want to make the claims that inflate a bubble. They obviously work.
