I keep running into products wearing "具身智能," or embodied intelligence, like a sticker, and it's starting to bug me. Nobody stops to notice what the phrase is actually claiming: an intelligence that happens to be embodied. The emphasis belongs on "intelligence," not "embodied."

Take 亲宝宝's "AI睡眠舱," an AI-branded baby crib that [went on sale in July 2026](https://cn.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202608/18/WS6a8416d0a3105d3d7a27b8fd.html), pitched as completing a "perception-decision-execution-feedback" (感知-决策-执行-反馈) closed loop entirely inside the crib. That's real embodied-intelligence vocabulary, applied to a crib. Whether it earns the label is a separate question, and it's the one worth asking. The crib's own marketing lists real sensing (cameras, infrared, pressure, radar) and real actions (soothing music or a story, a temperature alert, tracking a baby's growth), but none of that tells you whether the "decision" step in between is doing real work. Swap it for a fixed if-this-then-that rule — cry sound triggers lullaby, temperature threshold triggers alert — and I don't know whether the same actions would still fire on the same triggers.

Nobody's published that comparison, including me.

But that's the actual test, and "we don't know" is the point: the marketing asserts a closed loop with total confidence, and confidence is exactly what the vocabulary hasn't earned yet.

That's a narrower claim than "no intelligence, no loop." A thermostat has a feedback loop too: sensor, rule, actuator, sensor again. Nobody calls a thermostat embodied intelligence. The test isn't whether a loop exists in the engineering sense. It's whether the decision in the middle of that loop is actually intelligent, or just a preset trigger wearing a bigger word. If removing the model changes nothing about what the product does, the "intelligence" in "embodied intelligence" was never doing the work the name claims.

None of this happens in a vacuum. China's embodied-intelligence sector added [22 new ¥10-billion-plus unicorns in the first half of 2026 alone](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2026-07-14/doc-inihtvuh8554773.shtml). I can't tell you the word pulled that money in. Correlation isn't proof. But the vocabulary clearly wins attention, and everyone wants to be caught using it.

Where my own test gets less clean: once the thing driving the behavior is an unexplainably huge complex model, I'm honestly not sure the line holds. Something similar happened with large language models. Nobody voted on it, but at some point the models got big enough that people quietly stopped treating "intelligence" as a metaphor and started treating it as a description. Maybe a big enough model behind a body earns the same pass, whether or not the loop is doing the work I think it should. I don't have a settled answer, and I don't yet have one specific product where I can honestly say I can't tell if it's a real loop or a well-rehearsed demo. If you've got one, I want to hear it.

What bothers me about the current mess isn't only the crib. Embodied AI, physical AI, smart hardware, IoT, and plain robotics all get flattened into one word, and the flattening does real work: for a pitch deck, for a headline. And if I'm honest, there's a small sense of jealousy in there too: I don't want to make the claims that inflate a bubble. They obviously work.
